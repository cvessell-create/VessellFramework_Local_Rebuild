# VesselFramework Maskirovka Note — v3.8.1 (Public Release)
- Maskirovka checks are an original analytic extension developed by Christopher R. Vessell (2025–present). They are published openly under the Apache-2.0 license with the rest of VesselFramework.
- Grounding: the term borrows from Soviet/Russian military deception doctrine (Thomas, 2004; Grau & Bartles, 2016). The four checks below are VesselFramework extensions, not established doctrine, and should be evaluated on their own merits.
- Primary check: Brute-Force Stealth (developing) — tests whether saturation, persistence, and recurrence are being used to hide intent inside operational noise.
- Reference checks: Budgetary (spend pattern vs. stated mission), Structural (governing architecture certifies compliance while the mission requirement fails; does not require intent), Operational (legitimate-appearing activity concealing hostile intent or capability; requires evidence of that intent or capability).
- Evidence discipline: multi-variant convergence requires resolved, distinct provenance roots. Material unresolved lineage returns INDETERMINATE, not independent convergence.
- Use limits: a Maskirovka finding is an analytic hypothesis that must be tested, not an attribution or accusation. Do not apply the label to a named person, organization, or standard without source-established evidence, and state the strongest alternative explanation.
- External writing: lead with the target domain's accepted vocabulary and evidence structure. Cite framework labels with attribution when they are used.
- Disclosure: always meet academic-integrity, citation, source, and generative-AI disclosure requirements.
