# Preliminary Prospectus — Work-Life-Supportive Leadership and Employee Stress

**Status:** Early scoping document; not an approved proposal or protocol.  
**Research area:** Organizational psychology and psychologically healthy workplaces.

## Working title

*Work-Life-Supportive Leadership, Employee Involvement, Perceived Stress, and
Work–Nonwork Interference: A Proposed Organizational-Psychology Study*

## Candidate research question

Among employees in one clearly defined work setting, how are perceived
supervisor support for work-life needs and employee involvement associated with
perceived stress and work–nonwork interference?

This question is a starting point, not a claim that the relationships are
already established in the target population. The population and setting must
be selected with an advisor before specifying the sampling frame or measures.
“Work-life balance” is not treated as one self-evident variable: the protocol
must define whether it concerns conflict, enrichment, flexibility, boundary
control, or perceived balance.

## Rationale and conceptual boundaries

Grawitch, Gottschalk, and Munz (2006) organize healthy-workplace practices into
work-life balance, employee growth and development, health and safety,
recognition, and employee involvement. This study direction narrows that
broader framework to employee involvement and work-life-supportive leadership,
with perceived stress and work–nonwork interference as candidate outcomes.
The framework is a literature-based organizing model, not a validated causal
model for the proposed population.

WHO guidance recommends organizational interventions addressing psychosocial
risks, alongside manager and worker training and individual supports. That
guidance motivates attention to working conditions and organizational context;
it does not establish that a particular intervention will work in a particular
organization. Evidence-based decision making here means appraising relevant
research, contextual evidence, and stakeholder needs transparently rather than
transferring a generic “best practice” uncritically.

## Proposed design and limits

The least-assumptive starting design is an observational study of employee
reports. If a one-time survey is selected, analyses can describe distributions
and associations, but cannot establish temporal order or causal effects.
Common-method measurement, nonresponse, selection, organizational clustering,
and unmeasured confounding must be considered. A longitudinal or intervention
design would require a separate feasibility, power, and ethics assessment.

Before protocol approval, specify:

1. the workforce and unit of analysis;
2. operational definitions and established measures for each construct;
3. recruitment, sampling, expected response, and power rationale;
4. a preregistered or otherwise documented analysis plan, including missing
   data and subgroup analyses;
5. confidentiality protections that prevent employers or supervisors from
   identifying individual responses;
6. stakeholder input, consent, data minimization, retention, and secure access;
7. handling of adverse findings, uncertainty, and results that do not support
   the expected associations.

No employee data may be collected under this prospectus as written. Consult the
appropriate institutional review/ethics office and obtain required approvals
before recruitment or access to identifiable or sensitive workplace data.
Whether a project qualifies for review, exemption, or another determination is
for that institution—not this repository—to decide.

## Evidence and software boundary

The current repository contains no approved protocol, organizational-psychology
dataset, selected validated measures, analysis code, or empirical results for
this question. Existing cybersecurity, intelligence-analysis, verification,
and game artifacts are legacy software and examples; they are not evidence
about employee wellbeing. Software tests can establish that data handling and
calculations follow specifications. They cannot establish construct validity,
representativeness, causal effects, or intervention efficacy.

## Decisions for advisor review

- Is the question and proposed population sufficiently narrow and feasible?
- Which work-life construct and validated measure best fit the setting?
- Should employee involvement remain in scope, or should the study focus on
  one primary predictor?
- What design and analysis can answer the question without overclaiming?
- What approvals, privacy safeguards, and organizational permissions apply?
- Is a survey study appropriate, or should the project instead be a structured
  evidence review with no participant recruitment?
